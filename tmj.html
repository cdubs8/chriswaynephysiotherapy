What is a TMJ disorder?

Temporomandibular disorders (TMD), often called TMJ disorders, are problems with the jaw joint, the muscles that move it and the structures around it. You have one temporomandibular joint (TMJ) on each side of your face, where your jawbone meets your skull.

TMD can have many causes, including clenching or grinding, stress, an injury to the jaw or neck, arthritis, or changes in how the jaw and neck move together. Because the cause is different for each person, your treatment should be too.

Signs you may have one

TMD can show up in more than one way. Common signs include:

Jaw pain

An ache or sharp pain in or around the jaw, often worse when you chew or talk.

Clicking or locking

Clicking, popping or catching when you open and close your mouth, or a jaw that sticks.

Trouble chewing or opening wide

Difficulty biting into food, yawning or opening your mouth as far as you used to.

Headaches and facial pain

Headaches, earaches or pain in the temples and cheeks that seem linked to your jaw.

Neck and jaw tension

Tight, sore muscles in the neck and jaw, often with stiffness through the shoulders.

How we treat it

Chris is certified in the Goralsky Craniomandibular Method, a specialized approach to assessing and treating jaw pain and dysfunction. Your treatment is chosen after your assessment and may include:

Goralsky Craniomandibular Method

A specialized approach to assessing and treating jaw pain and dysfunction.

Manual therapy

Hands-on treatment for the jaw, neck and surrounding muscles to ease pain and tension.

Exercise

Gentle, progressive exercises to improve how your jaw moves and to build control.

Dry needling

Fine needles to help release tight muscles around the jaw and neck.

Education

Simple strategies for everyday habits such as posture, chewing and clenching, so you can manage symptoms between visits.

TMJ questions

Do I need a referral?

No. You can book directly with us. If you are claiming through insurance, check with your provider first in case they have their own requirements.

How many visits will I need?

It depends on the cause and how long you have had symptoms. You will get an honest estimate after your first visit, and we review it as you improve.

Will treatment be painful?

Most people find treatment comfortable. Some techniques can cause mild, short-lived soreness. We always explain what to expect and adjust to what feels right for you.

When should I go to the emergency room?

Go to the emergency room if your jaw is locked open or shut, or you think your jaw may be broken. If you are unsure, call 811 or your doctor.

Is TMJ treatment covered by insurance?

Many extended health plans cover physiotherapy, but coverage varies, so check your annual limit and what your plan requires. Our fees and coverage section has more detail.
